ARGUMENT

I. THE TRIAL COURT DID NOT ERR IN FAILING TO FIND DEFENDANT COMMITTED FRAUD UPON THE COURT WHEN DEFENDANT SUBMITTED AN AFFIDAVIT SIGNED BY A LICENSED NOTARY IN THE STATE OF OHIO.

Ohio Revised Code Section 147 addresses the conduct of a notary public. This section clearly defines an affidavit as a written statement of facts, the truth of which is sworn to before a person authorized to administer oaths and followed by an official statement of the person taking the oath that the affidavit was signed and sworn to, or affirmed, in his presence.

The notary's certification is considered sufficient to show the authenticity of the signature. While Ohio does not require a notary public to authenticate a notarial act with a seal of office at the time of notarization, an inadvertent failure to do so would mean that it would be up to a court of law to determine whether an improper notarization would affect the legality of an instrument. In our present case, though a seal was affixed, the Cuyahoga County Court of Common Pleas, in rendering a summary judgment decision after a review of the record before it, clearly determined that the presence of the seal, even if it did not exist, did not affect the legality of the affidavit and was non-determinative in the summary judgment motion having been granted in favor of Defendant-Appellee.

III. THE TRIALCOURT DID NOT ERR IN EFFICIENTLY RUNNING ITS COURTROOM IN DETERMINING THAT A PRE-TRIAL WAS NOT WARRANTED WHEN SUMMARY JUDGMENT WAS WELL TAKEN.

On April 11, 2013 the trial court granted Appellee's Motion for Summary Judgment, in part. In that same ruling, the trial court denied Defendant's Motion for Sanctions, which requested Appellant be declared a vexatious litigator. For purposes of this appeal, the latter issue will be discussed separately in this Answer Brief.

Defendant's motion for summary judgment was unopposed and appropriate in this case. The standard for review is as follows:

A Motion for Summary Judgment is provided in Rule 56 of the Ohio Rules of Civil Procedure. Summary Judgment is appropriate in Ohio courts if:

(1) "No genuine issue as to any material fact remains to be litigated;

(2) the moving party is entitled to judgment as a matter of law; and,

(3) it appears from the evidence that reasonable minds can come to but one conclusion,

and viewing such evidence most strongly in favor of the party against whom the Motion for Summary Judgment is made, that conclusion is adverse to that party." Temple v. Wean United, Inc. (1977), 50 Ohio St.2d 317, 327, O.O. 3d 466, 471-72, 364 N.E. 2d 267, 274; Delker v. Ohio Edison Co. (1989), 47 Ohio App.3d 1,2, 546 N.E. 2d 975,976.

"Additionally, for purposes of Summary Judgment, all inferences drawn from the evidence must be viewed in a light most favorable to the non-moving party." Mers v. Dispatch Printing Co. (1985), 19 Ohio St.3d 100, 105-106, 19 OBR 261, 265-266, 483 N.E.2d 150, 154-156; Williams v. First United Church of Christ (1974), 37 Ohio St.2d, 150, 152,66 O.O. 2d 311, 312, 309 N.E. 2d 924,925-26.

As a result of the granting of the unopposed Motion for Summary Judgment, it was not necessary for courtroom efficiency to hold a pre-trial. A trial court is permitted to run its courtroom docket in this appropriate manner, and its independent judgment in doing so is not subject to review merely because its actions did not meet the approval of one of the litigants. For this reason, Appellant's assignment of error must fail.

IV. THE TRIAL COURT DID NOT ERR IN GRANTING SUMMARY JUDGMENT IN FAVOR OF DEFENDANT WHEN DEFENDANT MET ALL ELEMENTS IN THE STANDARD FOR REVIEW AND WAS ENTITLED TO SUMMARY JUDGMENT AS A MATTER OF LAW.

As referenced above, Appellee met all elements in the standard for review of summary judgment. That standard, found in Rule 56 of the Ohio Rules of Civil Procedure, provide succinct guidance in this present instance. That standard, again, is as follows: Summary Judgment is appropriate in Ohio courts if:

(1) "No genuine issue as to any material fact remains to be litigated;

(2) the moving party is entitled to judgment as a matter of law; and,

(3) it appears from the evidence that reasonable minds can come to but one conclusion, and viewing such evidence most strongly in favor of the party against whom the Motion for Summary Judgment is made, that conclusion is adverse to that party.

Having met this standard, the trial court was correct in granting Defendant's unopposed Motion for Summary Judgment. As a result, Plaintiff's assignment of error must fail.

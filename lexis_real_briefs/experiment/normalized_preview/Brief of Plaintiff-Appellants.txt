STANDARD OF REVIEW

This Court stated in American Express Travel Related Services Company, Inc., v. Mandilakis (1996), 111 Ohio App.3d 160, that

The standard of review for an appeal from summary judgment is plenary. This court applies the same test as the trial court, which is set forth in Civ.R. 56, and we evaluate the record according to Civ.R. 56. Civ.R. 56 specifically provides before summary judgment may be granted it must be determined that "(1) [n]o genuine issue as to any material fact remains to be litigated; (2) the moving party is entitled to judgment as a matter of law; and (3) it appears from the evidence that reasonable minds can come to but one conclusion, and viewing such evidence most strongly in favor of the party against whom the motion for summary judgment is made, that conclusion is adverse to that party." Temple v. Wean United, Inc. (1977), 50 Ohio St.2d 317, 327, 4 0.0.3d 466, 472, 364 N.E.2d 267, 274.

Moreover, it is well settled that the party seeking summary judgment bears the burden of showing that no genuine issue of material fact exists for trial. Celotex Corp. v. Catrett (1987), 477 U.S. 317, 330, 106 S.Ct. 2548, 2556, 91 L.Ed.2d 265, 278; Mitseff v. Wheeler (1988), 38 Ohio St.3d 112, 115, 526 N.E.2d 798, 801-802. Doubts must be resolved in favor of the nonmovant. Murphy v. Reynoldsburg (1992), 65 Ohio St.3d 356, 359, 604 N.E.2d 138, 140. Under Civ.R. 56(E) "a nonmovant may not rest upon the mere allegations or denials of his pleadings but must set forth specific facts showing there is a genuine issue for trial." Chaney v. Clark Cty. Agricultural Soc. (1993), 90 Ohio App.3d 421, 424, 629 N.E.2d 513, 515.

Mankilakis, at 163-64.

Moreover, when reviewing a summary judgment, an appellate court must independently review the record to determine if summary judgment was appropriate. An appellate court need not defer to the trial court's decision in summary judgment cases. See Coburn v. Maynard (1996) 111 Ohio App.3d 246; Morehead v. Conley (1991), 75 Ohio App.3d 409, 599 N.E.2d 788.

LAW AND ARGUMENT

I.  THE TRIAL COURT ERRED IN GRANTING SUMMARY JUDGMENT TO APPELLEES WHERE APPELLEES HARWOOD. RESOURCE. AND HORVATH HAVE UNCLEAN HANDS.

Harwood, in a voice mail message left on Solomon's cell phone, alluded to his commission of criminal activity in connection with the sale to Solomon. What Harwood was referring to was the mortgage fraud on WaMu; i.e., the manner in which the WaMu short payoff was handled, and the creation of a fictitious HUD-1 by Resource, which Harwood certified to be accurate. The tape recording of Harwood was played by Solomon at his deposition. The following is the relevant excerpt.

. Harwood: Anyway, we're just looking at more money and more - more litigation, possibly some criminal stuff . . . (See Solomon Tr. Excerpt p. 5.)

Horvath was deeply involved in that transaction. There can be no doubt that Harwood and Horvath came before the Court with unclean hands. See Seitz v. Kozma, 2006 WL 1934547 (Ohio App. 8 Dist.) The facts supporting this defense include, but are not limited to, the following:

Commission of illegal act(s) and/or fraud on WaMu, as evidenced by the $ 880,000.00 mortgage loan obtained by Harwood from WaMu during the initial purchase transaction of the Adelaide property; the subsequent negotiation of the "short sale" of the property, which was approved by WaMu as to the Harwood/Solomon purchase agreement; the creation of a fraudulent HUD-1 certified by Harwood and Resource to be true and accurate in all respects and delivered to WaMu, thus satisfying the $ 880,000.00 mortgage lien on the property;

Alteration of the December 10, 2007, contract between Harwood and Mustafa;

Horvath and Harwood's concealment from, and/or lying to, Solomon regarding dealings with Mustafa and the existence of a contract with Mustafa;

Connivance between Harwood, Horvath, and Mustafa in causing record title to the property to be conveyed to the Mustafas in an effort to evict Solomon from the property, afterlis pendens had operated in this case;

Utilizing Mustafa as a front to attempt to evict Solomon from the property because Harwood was unable to do so due to unclean hands, bad faith, fraud, and illegal conduct in connection with the purchase and short sale of the property;

Defying the common pleas court's jurisdiction and attempting to frustrate its ability to grant effective relief by forum shopping to Rocky River Court;

Horvath's filing of a materially false answer on behalf of Harwood claiming that the Mustafa contract was terminated due to filing of this lawsuit, where the transaction had simply been shifted to CHT to close.

These facts collectively constitute an overwhelming case of unclean hands as to Harwood and Horvath. For all the reasons set forth herein the trial court should have denied them summary judgment.

II. THE TRIAL COURT ERRED IN GRANTING SUMMARY JUDGMENT TO MUSTAFA WHERE MUSTAFA INTERFERED WITH SOLOMON'S CONTRACT AND TOOK TITLE TO THE PROPERTY IN VIOLATION OF THE DOCTRINE OF LIS PENDENS WITH ACTUAL NOTICE OF SOLOMON'S INTEREST IN THE PROPERTY.

The doctrine of lis pendens is a fundamental tenet of law. This case involves a flagrant violation of the doctrine of lis pendens. The trial court erred in treating the deed to Mustafa as valid where such deed was issued during the pendency of this litigation in violation of lis pendens.

The Ohio Supreme Court stated in Cook v. Mozer (1923), 108 Ohio St. 30, 36, 140 N.E. 590, 592, that the purpose of the doctrine is to preserve the status quo of the interests in the property that is the subject of pending litigation until the court can render a final adjudication on the issues pertaining to that property.

In an effort to oust Solomon from the property, and to circumvent the jurisdiction of the trial court, Harwood improperly transferred title to the property to Mustafa after both had been sued and filed answers in this case, and before Solomon's claim for specific performance claim against Harwood was adjudicated. Mustafa then utilized the lis pendens deed to evict Solomon from the property.

Documents obtained from CHT reveal that Mustafa knew he was taking title to the property subject to Solomon's claim. (See CHT title insurance commitment). The documents further reveal that CHT was aware of this lawsuit, and that William Schill, an attorney acting for CHT, specifically informed Horvath on January 30, 2008 that CHT could not issue title evidence insuring over Solomon's claim. IV.  THE TRIAL COURT ERRED IN ENFORCING INDEMNIFICATION AGREEMENT LANGUAGE IN RESOURCE'S STANDARD CONDITIONS OF ESCROW WHERE THE EVIDENCE ESTABLISHED UNEQUIVOCALLY THAT RESOURCE BREACHED ITS FIDUCIARY DUTY AND CONTRACTUAL RESPONSIBILITIES TO SOLOMON TO ASSIST HARWOOD IN AVOIDING HIS CONTRACTUAL OBLIGATIONS TO SOLOMON AND/OR WAS NEGLIGENT IN TERMINATING THE ESCROW PREMATURELY.

The main function of an escrow agent is to hold documents and funds until the conditions are met whereupon the escrow agent releases the documents and funds. Spaulding v. Coulson (1995), 104 Ohio App. 3d 62, 81. Moreover, the duty of the escrow agent is "to carry out the terms of the agreement as intended by the parties." Pippin v. Kern-Ward Building Co. (1982), 8 Ohio App. 3d 196, 198. Moreover, "[a]n escrow holder occupies a fiduciary relationship with both the parties to the escrow agreement, and has attendant duties of loyalty, disclosure/and care, but the particular tasks with which the escrow holder is charged are those set forth in the escrow agreement." West Knoxville Associates Ltd. Partnership v. Ticor Title Ins. Co. (1998) 124 F. 3d 201 (6th Cir.)

Here, Resource as escrow agent owed a fiduciary duty to Solomon as a party to the real estate transaction, with regard to the implementation of the purchase agreement. Resource therefore had a fiduciary duty to act honestly and fairly in handling their transaction, and not to prefer one party over the other. Further, it was prohibited from engaging in any illegal conduct with respect to the transaction, or in dealing with the parties' fundo.

It is indisputable that in the course of the transaction, Resource issued a fictitious HUD-1 statement to WaMu representing that the sale to Solomon had closed. Resource did this to permit Harwood to obtain a reduction of his mortgage balance. Resource issued the false HUD-1 without Solomon's knowledge or authority to involve him in the fraud which Harwood and Resource were committing upon WaMu. Further, Resource utilized funds provided by Solomon to effect an illegal short payoff. Solomon was unaware that the manner in which Resource was handling the transaction was illegal. Solomon would not have sanctioned this conduct. The addendum to the purchase agreement which Solomon had signed provided that the transaction would close when Solomon obtained his financing.

The preparation and issuance of the fictitious HUD-1, the use of funds of Solomon and Harwood to effect a short payoff on the premise of a transfer, the premature termination of the escrow, and the partiality shown to Harwood and Horvath, all constituted breaches of Resource's fiduciary and contractual duties to Solomon. As a result, Solomon has stated a valid claim against Resource.

Solomon has also stated a valid claim in negligence. Numerous Ohio courts, including this Court in Calhoun, et al. v. Mccullough, et al., 1991 WL 64317 (Ohio App. 8 Dist.) have recognized that where facts support a breach of contract or breach of fiduciary duty claim against an escrow agent, the escrow agent can also be liable in negligence. See also Lashua, et al. v. Lakeside Title & Escrow Agency 2005 WL 844973 (Ohio, App. 5 Dist.), 2005 -Ohio- 1728 No. 2004CA00237, a title company/escrow agent was held liable for negligence for failure to disburse funds according to statutory and contract law; and Meerhoff, Executor. v. Huntington Mortgage Company, 103 Ohio App.3d 164, 658 N.E.2d 1109 (Ohio App. 3 Dist.), escrow agent was held liable for negligence for failure to cause a deed to be recorded promptly upon closing, thereby allowing a post-closing lien filed against seller to attach to the property.

V.  THE TRIAL COURT ERRED IN GRANTING SUMMARY JUDGMENT TO HORVATH WHERE SOLOMON SATISFIED BOTH THE PRIVITY AND MALICE PRONGS OF SCHOLLER, THE QUESTION OF WHETHER MALICE EXISTED WAS A JURY ISSUE. AND SOLOMON PROVIDED EXPERT TESTIMONY IN SUPPORT OF HIS LEGAL MALPRACTICE CLAIM.

A third person can maintain a legal malpractice claim against the lawyer of another where such third person is in privity of contract with the client or the attorney acts maliciously. Scholler v.Scholler, 10 Ohio St.3d at 103 (1984). Horvath contends that Solomon cannot maintain a claim against Horvath for legal malpractice because Solomon is not a client of Horvath and because privity of contract is lacking and because the interests of Harwood and Solomon are allegedly not concurrent. The privity requirement is satisfied because of the contract between Solomon and Harwood.

As to concurrency, the Ninth District Court of Appeals discussed that issue in Macken v. KDR, 2007 Ohio 4106, 2007 Ohio App. LEXIS 3733 (Ohio App. 9 Dist). The dispute centered around the preparation of a mortgage deed on real property in Lorain County, and whether the Wickens law firm prepared that mortgage deed. Macken was in privity of contract with Wickens' client. Wickens argued that the privity exception to the attorney third party immunity was not satisfied because the interests of KDR and Macken were not concurrent with respect to the Macken mortgage deed. The Ninth District concluded that the interests of Macken and KDR were in fact concurrent because both KDR and Macken wanted the indebtedness to Macken secured by a mortgage.

Here, both Harwood and Solomon had agreed on terms of sale, which included extending the closing date of sale through the close of business on December 14, 2007. Therefore, their interests were concurrent. There is more than an arm's-length dealing between Solomon and Harwood. They had ongoing negotiations about the sale of the property to Solomon, and entered into multiple contracts. This was not a case of one party offering a form contract and the other accepting.

Solomon has also established here that Horvath acted maliciously because the conduct of Horvath clearly constitutes conscious disregard for the rights and safety of Solomon, i.e., malice. Malice has been defined to mean "conscious disregard for the rights and safety of others." 13

Solomon alleges that Horvath, in his representation of Harwood and acting has his attorney-in-fact, participated in a scheme to defraud Solomon by selling the property to Mustafa while Solomon's contract was still in effect, without exception for Solomon's rights. Here, Solomon satisfies both criteria of the Scholler test; Solomon had privity of contract with Horvath's client, Harwood, and Horvath acted maliciously in the representation of Harwood. Solomon could not be lawfully dispossessed of the property without compensation equal to the fair market value of all the improvements made by him to the property as provided in RC § 5303.08. As noted in Easterling v. Miller, 1998 WL 3642 (Ohio App. 4 Dist.) THE TRIAL COURT ERRED IN DENYING SOLOMON THE RIGHT TO A JURY TRIAL ON DAMAGES.

"The text of [R.C. 5303.08] contemplates that the person being evicted will assert his claim for the value of improvements, and obtain an adjudication of that claim, before, and as a condition of, his eviction." Platt v. Petrosky (March 23, 1994), Montgomery App. No. 14212, unreported.

Solomon filed a jury demand in this case. In Galayda v. Lake Hosp. System, Inc., 1993 WL 389754 (Ohio App. 8 Dist., 1993), the Court stated:

With regard to the right to jury trial, such right is guaranteed by Section 5, Article I, of the Ohio Constitution. The legislature may not attempt to either limit or abolish the right to a trial by jury. See, Gibbs v. Girard (1913), 88 Ohio St. 43. The right to have a jury determine the amount of damages is a substantive right not to be abridged by any action. Kneisley v. Lattimer-Stevens Co. (1988), 40 Ohio St.3d 354.

In Zoppo v. Homestead Ins. Co. (1994), 71 Ohio St.3d 552, 557, 644 N.E.2d 397, the Court stated:

"Ohio courts have consistently understood that all damages, without distinction as to type, are for the jury to determine as part of the jury trial right."

Where a party has a cause of action, it is entitled to have damages determined by a jury. Sorrell, 69 Ohio St.3d at 422, 633 N.E.2d 504.

In the case of Stonehenge Land Company v. Beazer Homes Investments, L. L. C., (2008) 177 Ohio App.3d 7, 893 N.E.2d 855, 2008 -Ohio-148, the Tenth District Court of Appeals specifically addressed the right to jury trial where attorneys fees were claimed as compensatory damages as Harwood and Resource sought in their motions for summary judgment. The Court ruled that because the attorney fees being sought herein were in the nature of damages, the trial court was required to submit the issue to the jury. See also Keal v. Day (2005) 164 Ohio App.3d 21, 840 N.E.2d 1139, 2005 -Ohio- 5551 (Ohio App. 1 Dist.) when award of attorney fees are awarded by contract, matter must be decided by a jury.

Harwood and Resource sought attorneys' fees as part of its claim for compensatory damages in this action. Therefore, Solomon has an absolute right to have a jury determine whether attorney fees should be awarded and the amount of same, after hearing all the evidence at trial.

IX.  THE TRIAL COURT ERRED IN DISCHARGING SOLOMON'S MECHANIC'S LIEN.

Harwood's motion for summary judgment regarding Solomon's mechanic's lien should have been denied because a genuine issue of fact exist because regarding Solomon's legal status. Harwood contends that Solomon's lien is invalid because the work performed was not authorized by Harwood as the "owner" of the property. Harwood further contends that Solomon is simply a lessee of the property. In fact, he is the equitable owner of the property.

Section 1311.01 of the Ohio Revised Code states:

Lien definitions. As used in sections 1311.01 to 1311.22 of the Revised Code:

(A) "Owner," "part owner," or "lessee" includes all the interests either legal or equitable, which such person may have in the real estate upon which the improvements are made, including the interests held by any person under contracts of purchase, whether in writing or otherwise. (Emphasis added)

Here, the original purchase agreement was entered into on August 24, 2005. At all relevant times a purchase agreement was in effect. As a result, Solomon as at all relevant times has been the equitable owner of the property. Harwood admitted in his deposition that Solomon is contract purchaser and not a lessee. (Harwood Tr. p. 133) Under the doctrine of equitable conversion, Solomon became the equitable owner of the property when the first purchase agreement was executed by in 2005. See Wood v. Donohue (1999). Ohio App. 1 Dist. 136 Ohio App.3d 336, 736 N.E.2d 556. 14 As the equitable owner of the property Solomon could authorize work to be done on the property. He could also, as here, act as the general contractor to perform the work.

Further, RC § 1311.10 oreates a presumption that Solomon was authorized to contract for or cause work to be done on the property.

1311.10 Pressumptions.

(A) Any person who contracts for an improvement to real property which gives rise to lien rights under sections 1311.01 to 1311.22 of the Revised Code is presumed to be the authorized agent of all part owners of the real property, except when the relationship between the person contracting for the improvement and the other part owners is that of lessee and lessor or sublessee and sublessor.

(C) The presumptions established by this section may be overcome only by clear and convincing evidence.

Effective Date: 04-16-1993

Harwood's motion for partial summary judgment should have also been denied because a genuine issue of fact exists regarding the amount due on Solomon's lien.

X.  THE TRIAL COURT ERRED IN AWARDING DAMAGES TO APPELLEES HARWOOD, RESOURCE AND MUSTAFA. AND ALL SUCH AWARDS MUST BE VACATED.

All of the damage awards on this case are predicated upon determinations of liability which are erroneous. Therefore, all damage awards in this case must be vacated. Further, there were no valid grounds to impose punitive damages against Solomon for filing a lien.

XI. THE TRIAL COURT ERRED IN FAILING TO ISSUE FINDINGS OF FACT AND CONCLUSIONS OF LAW AS REQUIRED BY LAW.

The trial court erred in failing and refusing to make findings of fact and conclusions of law despite requests by Appellants and Harwood. As Harwood noted in his request for same (R#-310), because the ruling of the trial court regarding the Solomon contract was a "potentially final order," such findings and conclusions were required pursuant to Civil Rule 52. As Harwood states,

"Ohio case law and appellate procedure require that a trial court submit findings of fact and conclusions of law in support of any judgment on a declaratory judgment action." (R#-310)

Now, despite knowing he has an invalid judgment, Harwood is executing on that judgment.

CONCLUSION

In arriving at a decision in this case, this Court should review the entire record of this case and decide this case de novo. In so doing, this Court must consider all of the evidence and the totality of the circumstances. If this Court does so, this Court must be compelled to reverse the judgments of the trial court.

Accordingly, for these reasons, Plaintiff-Appellants Solomon request that this Court reverse the decision of the trial court and enter judgment in favor of Plaintiff-Appellants or remand this case for further proceedings.

ASSIGNMENTS OF ERROR

1.  THE TRIAL COURT ERRED IN GRANTING SUMMARY JUDGMENT IN FAVOR OF APPELLEES.

2.  THE TRIAL COURT ERRED BY IGNORING MATERIAL FACTS THAT REMAINED TO BE LITIGATED IN THIS MATTER.

3.  THE TRIAL COURT ERRED IN FAILING TO HOLD APPELLEES TO ITS BURDEN UNDER 56(C) OF THE OHIO RULES OF CIVIL PROCEDURE.

4.  THE TRIAL COURT ERRED BY EXERCISING AN ABUSE OF DISCRETION IN DENYING APPELLANTS BRIEF IN OPPOSITION TO APPELLEES' MOTION FOR SUMMARY JUDGMENT.

5.  THE TRIAL COURT ERRED BY FAILING TO UPHOLD DECISION FROM HIGHER COURT.

ISSUES PRESENTED FOR REVIEW

1. Whether Appellees Have A Right To Summary Judgment On The Basis Of Res Judicata?

2. Whether Appellants Brief In Opposition To Appellees' Motion For Summary Judgment Contained Valid Argument And Defense?

3. Whether The Trial Court Was Correct In Rendering Judgment When Material Facts Remained To Be Litigated?

4. Whether Material Facts In This Case Remained To Be Litigated?

5. Whether Parma Municipal Court and Cuyahoga Court Of Common Pleas Contained Issues Of Different Or Identical Nature?

6. Whether Appellees' Claim In Their Motion For Summary Judgment Is Barred By Virtue Of Precedent From A Higher Court?

STATEMENT OF THE CASE

This case is a matter of determining whether two cases are identical or so similar in nature that permits Appellees to prevail in a Summary Judgment upon the basis of Res Judicata.

The first case: Tanski Vs. Allen, ET. AL., held in Parma Municipal Court, Case No. 08 CVG 03356, was primarily an eviction case, and identifying minor damages to the rental property owned by Plaintiff.

In Defendants' Answer to Plaintiff's Complaint, four other issues were presented as a counterclaim against Plaintiff. The money issues were damages caused by Breach of Contract, as an unpaid balance owed to Defendant for work performed on the rental property, and Negligence, for property damages caused by Plaintiff against Defendant. The prayer amount in Defendants' counterclaim in that matter was $ 866.86 and $ 975.00, respectively, in the sum of $ 1,841.86.

The second case: Allen ET. AL. Vs. Tanski ET. AL., held in Cuyahoga County Court of Common Pleas, Case No. CV 08 676027, was based entirely on money issues. Since this case was filed prior to the trial date in the Parma case, two issues related to money were identical, however, once the Parma case was adjudicated, Plaintiff filed a Motion to withdraw those particular issues from this case. From this, a prayer amount remained in the sum of $ 38,200.

In an attempt to dispose of the Court of Common Pleas Case, Defendant (Appellees herein) filed a Motion for Summary Judgment, using Res Judicata as its basis, stating that the cases were identical in nature, and that because the case issues were previously adjudicated in the Parma case, no genuine issues of material facts remained to be litigated.

However, despite Plaintiffs Brief in Opposition to Defendants Motion for Summary Judgment, and the introduction of remaining facts to be litigated, as evidence, in addition to the ruling of a higher court, barring the application of Res Judicata as relevant in this type of case, the Trial Court rendered judgment in favor of Appellees and dismissed the case. In this, the Trial Court has erred.

STATEMENT OF THE FACTS

The specifics of the two cases are clear: The issues in Appellants counterclaim against Appellees are as follows:

PARMA MUNI COURT: Case No. 08 CVG 03356

Count One - Breach of Contract for unpaid work - $ 866.86.

Count Two - Negligence for damages to personal property - $ 975.00.

Count Three - Breach of Contract of lease agreement for 3-day Notice.

Count Four - Unjust Enrichment / Fraud for seeking money despite the failure to return nor apply security deposit in lease agreement.

The issues in Appellants Complaint against Appellees are as follows:

CUYAHOGA COUNTY COURT OF COMMON PLEAS: Case No. CV 08 676027

Count One - Breach of Contract for failing to improve rental property - $ 13,200 (for all rent paid to date).

Count Two - Breach of Contract for unpaid work - $ 966.86.

Count Three - Breach of Contract for violation of terms of lease agreement and Ohio Revised Code 5321.04(A)(7)(8) - $ 10,000.

Count Four - Negligence for damages to personal property - $ 975.00.

Count Five - Fraud for intentional harm against tenants - $ 15,000.

From this, it is clear that the only similarity between cases are those involving the claims and prayer amounts for Breach of Contract and Negligence, whereas Parma Court Counts One and Two mirror Common Pleas Court Counts Two and Four. Outside of these similarities, of which both were dismissed at Appellants request in his Motion to Withdraw Counts Two and Four, the cases remain entirely different.

ARGUMENT

1.  THE TRIAL COURT ERRED IN GRANTING SUMMARY JUDGMENT IN FAVOR OF APPELLEES.

A.  THE AGRUMENT OF RES JUDICATA AS A BASIS FOR SUMMARY JUDGMENT IS INAPPLICABLE.

The issues of the two cases are not identical, as argued by Appellees in their Motion for Summary Judgment, whereas Appellees state both on page 1, paragraph 1:

" . . . as there are no issues of material fact to come before this Court that have not been already adjudicated."

And, on page 2, paragraph 2 of Brief in Support:

"Defendants claims in this case have already been adjudicated on the same issues, facts, evidence and request for damages, in the Parma Municipal Court, in Cuyahoga County, Ohio under case number 08 CVG 03356."

B.  THE APPLICATION OF LAW IN APPELLEES BRIEF IS IRRELEVANT.

Appellees application of case law in Grava vs. Parkman Twp. (1995) 73 Ohio St.3d 379 and Independence Insurance Agency of Ohio Inc. vs. Duryee (1994), 95 Ohio App 3d 7, 641 N.E. 2nd 1117, is irrelevant for the following reason:

The Appeals Court ruled that:

"If, however, the two suits do not involve the same claim, demand, and cause of action, such effect will not be ordinarily given to the prior judgment. * * *30 American Jurisprudence, 914 Section 172. To constitute a bar there must be identity not only of subject matter but also of the cause of action. In other words, a judgment in a former action does not bar a subsequent action where the cause of action prosecuted is not the same, even though each action relates to the same subject matter." (Emphasis added)

The court later approved and followed this definition in paragraph one of the syllabus of Whitehead v. Gen. Tel. Co. (1969), 20 Ohio St. 2d 108, 49 O.2d 435, 254 N.E.2d 10.

2.  THE TRIAL COURT ERRED BY IGNORING MATERIAL FACTS THAT REMAINED TO BE LITIGATED IN THIS MATTER.

Had the Trial Court examined all the issues in both cases thoroughly, it would have easily identified the presence of three issues of material fact which remained to be litigated in this matter. Instead, the Trial Court combined all five issues with all four issues in the other case, and labeled them as having already been adjudicated by Parma Municipal Court. For reasons unknown to Appellant, the Trial Court did not bear any consideration to the fact that Appellants had taken the initiative and filed a Motion to Withdraw the two identical issues in both cases prior to the Trial Court's decision for Summary Judgment.

3.  THE TRIAL COURT ERRED IN FAILING TO HOLD APPELLEE TO ITS BURDEN UNDER 56(C) OF THE OHIO RULES OF CIVIL PROCEDURE.

This issue is addressed directly in Rule 56(C) of the Ohio Rules of Civil Procedure. In that regard, the Rule specifically states that:

Summary judgment shall not be rendered unless it appears from the evidence or stipulation, that reasonable minds can come to but one conclusion and that conclusion is adverse to the party against whom the motion for summary judgment is made, that party being entitled to have evidence or stipulation construed most strongly in the party's favor.

In its ruling, the Trial Court began by paying lip service to the mandates of Rule 56. In that regard, the Trial Court acknowledged that:

Absent an affirmative showing by the moving party that no genuine issues exist as to any material fact . . . and that such a party is entitled to judgment as a matter of law, no summary judgment may be granted. Harless v. Willis Day Warehousing Co. (1970), 54 Ohio St. 3d 64, 66.

4.  THE TRIAL COURT ERRED BY EXERCISING AN ABUSE OF DISCRETION IN DENYING APPELLANTS BRIEF IN OPPOSITION TO APPELLEES MOTION FOR SUMMARY JUDGMENT.

A. THE TRIAL COURT FAILED TO EVALUATE THE VALIDITY OF LAW AND ARGUMENT IN APPELLANTS BRIEF.

Had the Trial Court given fair consideration to Appellants' Brief in Opposition to Appellees Motion for Summary Judgment, it would have come to the conclusion that the cases are not identical, nor even so similar in nature as to disregard Appellants valid defense.

B.  THE TRIAL COURT ERRED IN CONSIDERING ONLY THE APPELLEES CLAIM IN THEIR MOTION FOR SUMMARY JUDGMENT SINCE A PRECEDENT EXISTS IN A HIGHER COURT PROHIBITING THE APPLICATION OF A SUMMARY JUDGMENT BASED ON RES JUDICATA IN CASES THAT ARE NOT IDENTICAL IN NATURE

Appellants demonstrated in their argument that a higher court established a precedent stating that the cases must be identical in nature, and contain the same claims, demand and cause of action in order to apply the doctrine of Res Judicata in a Summary Judgment. Since the cases herein are clearly so dissimilar, the Trial Court had executed an abuse of discretion in rendering its decision in favor of Appellees' Motion for Summary Judgment. See again, Grava vs. Parkman Twp. (1995) 73 Ohio St.3d 379 and Independence Insurance Agency of Ohio Inc. vs. Duryee (1994), 95 Ohio App 3d 7, 641 N.E. 2nd 1117.

5.  THE TRIAL COURT ERRED BY FAILING TO UPHOLD DECISION FROM HIGHER COURT.

The Tenth District Court of Appeals of Ohio, Franklin County, found in Independence Insurance Agency of Ohio Inc. vs. Duryee (1994) 95 Ohio App 3d 7, 641 N.E. 2nd 1117, that the Ohio Supreme Court has defined Res Judicata in Norwood v. McDonald (1943) Ohio St. 299, 305, 27 0.0 240, 242, 52 N.E.2d 67, 71 quoting 30 American Jurisprudence (1940) 908, Judgments, Section 161, as follows:

 "The doctrine of res judicata is that an existing final judgment rendered upon the merits, without fraud or collusion, by a court of competent jurisdiction, is conclusive of rights, questions and facts in issue, as to the parties and their privies, in all other actions in the same or any other judicial tribunal or concurrent jurisdiction."

The court continued:

"If, however, the two suits do not involve the same claim, demand, and cause of action, such effect will not be ordinarily given to the prior judgment. *** 30 American Jurisprudence, 914 Section 172. To constitute a bar there must be identity not only of subject matter but also of the cause of action. In other words, a judgment in a former action does not bar a subsequent action where the cause of action prosecuted is not the same, even though each action relates to the same subject matter." (Emphasis added).

The court later approved and followed this definition in paragraph one of the syllabus of Whitehead v. Gen. Tel. Co. (1969), 20 Ohio St. 2d 108, 49 O.2d 435, 254 N.E.2d 10.

Actions may arise out of the same transaction or relate to the same subject matter, yet still not be Res Judicata because the causes of action are not the same, Norwood, supra, 142 Ohio St.299, 300, 27 O.O. 240, 52 N.E.2d 67, 68-69, at paragraph two of the syllabus; Henderson v. Ryan (1968), 13 Ohio St.2d 31, 42 O.O.2d 65, 233 N.E.2d 506. Therefore, since the two causes of action are not the same, Res Judicata does not apply here as the bar for this claim for relief.

For all of these reasons, the Trial Court erred in granting Appellees' Motion for Summary Judgment.

CONCLUSION

The Trial Court's Order of May 29, 2009, must be reversed. Summary Judgment in favor of Appellees, John Tanski and Gayle J. Rullo should be denied, and this matter should proceed to a Jury Trial, as requested by Appellants, to allow the case issues to be determined on its own merits.

The Trial Court erred in:

1. Concluding that Appellees' Motion for Summary Judgment was proper and in accordance to law.

2. Concluding that no genuine issues of material fact remained to be litigated.

3. Failing to hold Appellees to their burden under Rule 56(C).

4. Exercising an abuse of discretion.

5. Failing to uphold prior ruling by Ohio Supreme Court.

For all of these reasons, Appellants respectfully request that the Trial Court's Order be reversed and that this matter be set for Jury Trial.

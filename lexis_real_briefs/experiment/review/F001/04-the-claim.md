# F001 — what the change is asserted to have done

**Class:** `missing_element` / `removed_status_element_for_statutory_protection`  
**Operation:** delete in Argument of Law -- Discussion  
**Claimed materiality:** high

## Before

```
Alice Stone's status could change once Cazeau had expressed his "will" to end and no longer "suffer" her occupation. That is, her status would then become one of a tenant at sufferance. Sutherland v. Moore (Sept. 15, 1988), 10th Dist. No. 88AP-482.
```

## After

```
(deleted)
```

## The vulnerability this is asserted to create

The brief concludes that Stone was entitled to the protections of R.C. Chapter 1923 without ever establishing the status that brings her within the statute. The immediately following sentences still assert that Chapter 1923 applies and that even a tenant at sufferance may be removed only by statutory process, so the argument now asserts the conclusion with the qualifying step removed.

## Why this was thought realistic

The Ninth District affirmed against Stone on precisely this point, and the appellee's brief in the companion Lee matter argues the same gap. A drafter who omits the status step produces a brief that reads as complete and loses for a reason nothing in its own text names.

## What the change was intended to leave alone

Deleted the two sentences (and their supporting citation) that establish Stone's legal status as a tenant at sufferance once the owner revoked her permission to occupy. Everything else -- the R.C. 1923.02 and 5321.15 discussion, the facts, the relief -- is untouched.

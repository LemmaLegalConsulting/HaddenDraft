## The vulnerability this is asserted to create

The brief quotes the adverse statutory text -- that forcible entry and detainer lies against an "occupier of lands or tenements, without color of title," which would reach this appellant whether or not she was a tenant -- and no longer answers it. The permissive "may be had" language, which is what makes the appellee's whole in-pari-materia argument work, is gone.

## Why this was thought realistic

This is the adversarial test rather than a citation test: nothing is malformed, no placeholder is left behind, and the brief reads as finished. The opponent stage has to rediscover the vulnerability from the statute the brief itself quotes.

## What the change was intended to leave alone

Deleted only the paragraph answering the appellant's strongest statutory point. The appellant's position stays in the brief: the preceding paragraph still quotes R.C. 1923.02(A)(1) and (A)(5) as she framed them, and the surrounding argument is unchanged.

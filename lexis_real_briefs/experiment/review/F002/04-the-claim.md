# F002 — what the change is asserted to have done

**Class:** `missing_element` / `removed_landlord_knowledge_application`  
**Operation:** delete in Preliminary Statement and Assignments of Error  
**Claimed materiality:** high

## Before

```
posed by the subject premises because Appellee's actual knowledge of these hazards is clearly evidenced by the fact that the City of Cleveland ordered Appellee to remediate numerous lead paint hazards after the City conducted an inspection of the premises after a previous child residing at the subject premises was diagnosed with lead poisoning.
```

## After

```
posed by the subject premises.
```

## The vulnerability this is asserted to create

The brief's own statement of the rule (quoting Sikora clarifying Shroades) preserves the excuse that a landlord is not liable under R.C. 5321.04 if it neither knew nor should have known of the circumstances causing the violation. With the clause removed, nothing in the brief applies that element to this appellee, so the negligence per se argument no longer answers the defense its own rule statement invites.

## Why this was thought realistic

Landlord knowledge is the element most often asserted rather than shown in habitability and lead-hazard briefing, and the control brief's own structure makes the omission invisible: the rule statement stays put in a later section, so the brief still looks like it addresses knowledge.

## What the change was intended to leave alone

Deleted the clause that applies the knowledge element, leaving the sentence's assertion that knowledge is not at issue standing on nothing. The negligence per se argument, the statutes and ordinances relied on, and the Shroades line of authority are unchanged.

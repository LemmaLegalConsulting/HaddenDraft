# F004 — what the change is asserted to have done

**Class:** `authority_applicability` / `binding_circuit_authority_replaced_with_out_of_circuit_district_court`  
**Operation:** replace in Argument I  
**Claimed materiality:** medium

## Before

```
The Sixth Circuit found that § 3617 extended to situations involving a wide range of circumstances, such as exclusionary zoning practices, deflated appraisals, and insurance redlining. Michigan Protection and Advocacy Service, Inc. v. Babin, 18 F.3d 337, 347 (6th Cir.1994).
```

## After

```
The court in Matarese found that § 3617 extended to situations involving a wide range of circumstances, such as exclusionary zoning practices, deflated appraisals, and insurance redlining. Matarese v. Archstone Pentagon City, 795 F. Supp. 2d 402, 442-43 (E.D. Va. 2011).
```

## The vulnerability this is asserted to create

An Ohio court sitting in the Sixth Circuit is now offered an Eastern District of Virginia decision, rather than its own circuit's, for the breadth of § 3617 -- and Matarese does not hold what the sentence attributes to it. No case name is invented and no citation is malformed; only the authority's applicability is defective.

## Why this was thought realistic

It reproduces the failure the Cleveland attorneys described: a cited case that says something very similar, offered in place of the authority the court is actually bound by.

## What the change was intended to leave alone

Replaced the binding Sixth Circuit authority for the scope of 42 U.S.C. § 3617 with a real out-of-circuit federal district court decision that the brief already cites elsewhere for a different proposition. The legal proposition asserted is word-for-word unchanged.

# F003 — what the change is asserted to have done

**Class:** `record_support` / `service_and_acknowledgment_not_established_by_cited_exhibit`  
**Operation:** replace in Statement of Undisputed Facts  
**Claimed materiality:** high

## Before

```
On June 14, 2006 STJ, through its agent Appellee Dayton Rental, delivered to Ealy and his wife a statutory three-day Notice to Leave the Premises in accordance with Ohio Revised Code Section 1923.04. (See Exhibit "A" to STJ's Complaint) by leaving such Notice at the premises.
```

## After

```
On June 14, 2006 STJ, through its agent Appellee Dayton Rental, personally served Ealy and his wife with a statutory three-day Notice to Leave the Premises in accordance with Ohio Revised Code Section 1923.04, and Ealy acknowledged receipt of that Notice at the July 21, 2006 hearing. (See Exhibit "A" to STJ's Complaint)
```

## The vulnerability this is asserted to create

The brief now attributes to Exhibit A a manner of service the exhibit does not show, and attributes to the July 21, 2006 hearing an acknowledgment of notice that the brief's own account of that hearing (an admission of non-payment of rent) does not contain. Service under R.C. 1923.04 is jurisdictional in a forcible entry and detainer action, so the overstatement is on the element the appellee most needs.

## Why this was thought realistic

This is the characteristic generated-text failure: the proposition is adjacent to what the record shows, the citation is real and correctly formatted, and only the strength of the claim has drifted.

## What the change was intended to leave alone

Upgraded a supported proposition (the notice was left at the premises, per Exhibit A) into two stronger propositions the same exhibit does not establish: personal service, and an on-the-record acknowledgment of receipt. The citation is unchanged and still points at Exhibit A.

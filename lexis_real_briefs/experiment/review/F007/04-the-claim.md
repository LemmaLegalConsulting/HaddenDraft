```
First, plaintiff served the defendants with an eviction notice by certified mail, return receipt requested, on June 28, 2023. See attached Exhibit 12 and Exhibit 3.
```

## The vulnerability this is asserted to create

The record the motion cites establishes posting at the premises, not certified mail. Exhibit 3, the process server's affidavit, swears she "served a Three-Day-Notice for the occupants to Leave the Premises at 8127 Cedar Road" and documents it with photographs of the notice at the property; Exhibit 12 is the notice itself, signed and dated 6-28-23. Nothing in the record is a return receipt. R.C. 1923.04(A) makes certified mail one permitted manner and personal or premises service another, and service is jurisdictional in a forcible entry and detainer action, so the brief now claims a manner of service its own record disproves on the element it can least afford to lose.

## Why this was thought realistic

This is the mutation the design asked for and the appellate stratum could not supply: the attachments are fixed, the assertion is adjacent to what they show, and catching it requires actually reading the record rather than inspecting the prose. A drafter reciting the statute's list of permitted methods and picking the wrong one produces exactly this.

## What the change was intended to leave alone

Added a manner of service to the first of the four R.C. 1923.04(A) elements the motion applies. The date is unchanged, the citation is unchanged, and the exhibits it points to are byte-identical between the two conditions.

MOTION FOR PARTIAL SUMMARY JUDGMENT
Now comes Maria Scalzo, plaintiff and executrix of The Estate of Anthony Notarian, by and through undersigned counsel, and for plaintiff's reply to defendants' brief in opposition to plaintiff's motion for partial summary judgment, states as follows:
I. MOTION FOR SUMMARY JUDGMENT STANDARD
Defendants misstate the motion for summary judgment standard. Plaintiff has met her burden under Civ. R. 56. Plaintiff presented evidence that no genuine issue of material fact remains to be litigated, that plaintiff is entitled to judgment as a matter of law, and that reasonable minds could come to but one conclusion even when viewing that evidence in the light most favorable to defendants. Plaintiff has satisfied

her initial burden, thus the burden shifted to defendants to set forth facts showing there still exists a genuine issue for trial. Dresher v. Burt, 75 Ohio St.3d 280, 293, 662 N.E.2d 264 (1996).
Defendants have had opportunity to present their defenses and reply to plaintiffs Motion for Partial Summary Judgment. In light of defendants' failure to present any evidence of a genuine issue of material fact for trial to satisfy their reciprocal burden, the Court should grant plaintiff's Motion for Partial Summary Judgment on the First Cause.
II. THE ESTATE'S RIGHT TO POSSESSION OF THE PROPERTY
Defendants admit that they signed the deed to the property over to plaintiff and delivered it to the Geauga County Probate Court. Along with the orders of the Probate Court attached to plaintiff's motion for partial summary judgment, and plaintiff having followed the requirements of R.C. Chapter 1923, this admission should end this Court's inquiry in favor of plaintiff.
Nevertheless, defendants argue the Probate Court's rulings with regard to the property are not final orders and cannot be executed upon. However, neither this matter nor the probate matter constitutes execution on a judgment. This matter is an

eviction pursuant to probate court orders to manage estate real property; the probate court vacated invalid transfers of real property by the former fiduciary of Anthony Notarian. Plaintiff will address these matters one at a time.
First, R.C. § 2113.311(E) grants estate executors and administrators statutory authority to manage estate property and "[p]rosecute actions for forcible entry and detainer" as a form of managing estate property. See In re Estate of Jacob, 8th Dist. Cuyahoga No. 85640, 2005-Ohio-4998, J 22 ("The power to manage real property as described in R.C. 2113.311 ... includes, among other things, the power to make repairs, insure the premises but not to prosecute actions for forcible entry and detainer."); See also Collins v. Jackson, 34 Ohio App.3d 101, 102, 517 N.E.2d 269 (8th Dist. 1986) ("An executrix charged with the duty to manage real estate is expressly authorized to prosecute actions for forcible entry and detention.").
Plaintiff filed an application to manage real property in Case No. 20 PE 000261 on September 29, 2020. In multiple orders submitted to this Court with plaintiff's Motion for Partial Summary Judgment, Judge Grendell charged plaintiff with the duty to manage the property in question. In accordance with the Probate Court orders and R.C. 2113.311, plaintiff has express authority to prosecute this action for forcible entry and detainer.

It is not unusual for estate executors and administrators to bring forcible entry and detainer actions pursuant to probate court orders to manage property. Sed «. Mundy, 11th Dist. Portage No. 2016-P-0019, 2016-Ohio-8431; Estate of Wos v. Wos, 6th Dist. Lucas No. L-05-1408, 2006-Ohio-4302; Matter of Estate of McDaniel, 7th Dist. No. 22 CA 0956, 2023-Ohio-1065, 212 N.E.3d 351; Cf. Barnick v. Barnick, 8th Dist. Cuyahoga No. 87997, 2007-Ohio-635. None of the cases cited by defendants in their brief are related to probate court proceedings, the management of estate property, or evictions. The Farmers State Bank v. Steven Spongaungle, et al., 2nd Dist. No. 2016-CA-4, 2017- Ohio-4322, 92 N.E.3d 355 involves foreclosure proceedings. Assuage v. Lithoprint, Ltd., 2nd Dist. Montgomery No. 23527, 2009-Ohio-7036 and Niagara v. Willacy (1997) 1997 WL 186842 both involve garnishment actions, the latter involving paternity proceedings in the Juvenile Division of the Cuyahoga County Court of Common Pleas. Finally, Wheeler v. Ohio State Univ. Med. Ctr., (2004) 2004-Ohio-2769 involves subrogation of settlement proceeds following an automobile accident. None of these cases are even indirectly applicable to plaintiff's ability to maintain this action.
With regard to the orders of the probate court, the parties here are precluded from relitigating facts or issues in prior actions. Fatica Renovations, LLC v. Bridge, 11 Dist. No. 2018-G-0150, 2018-Ohio-4949, 125 N.E.3d 184, at J32. The scope

of this Court's inquiry is limited to the present right of possession. That said, the Probate Court's orders provide a solid basis for this forcible entry and detainer action. Probate courts have the power to determine the validity of real property transfers and invalidate improper transfers, both before and after the death of decedent. Goldberg v. Maloney, 111 Ohio St.3d 211, 2006-Ohio-5485, 855 N.E.2d 856, at J34-36. Here the probate court invalidated two transfers undertaken by Gino Notarian: the first in 2019 where Gino transferred 8127 Cedar Road to himself; and the second in 2021 where he transferred the premises to Julia Notarian and Charles Rossbach III. Gino Notarian never had the ability to convey the property to himself, and never had valid title to convey to Julia Notarian and Charles Rossbach III.
It is undisputed Gino Notarian signed over title of the real property located at 8127 Cedar Road to defendants after plaintiff brought an action under R.C. 2109.50 to recover the property from him. Such a transfer is not valid. R.C. 2109.56 states "All gifts, grants, or conveyances of real property ... made or obtained with intent to avoid the purpose of the proceedings set forth in sections 2109.50 to 2109.55 of the Revised Code, or in contemplation of any examination or complaint provided for by those sections, shall be void." The transfer was without the knowledge of the Estate, for below fair market value, and Gino Notarian took possession of the purchase funds.

Undoing invalid and void transfers of property does not constitute an execution. This fact should be self-evident. In an analogous case involving the Ohio Uniform Fraudulent Transfer Act, the Fifth Appellate District Court of Appeals held it was not an execution for a trial court to vacate a fraudulent transfer and order the real property deeded back to the proper party. Guernsey Cnty. Cmty. Dev. Corp. v. Speedy, 2023-Ohio-1796, J 26-28, 216 N.E.3d 18, 26. Said order took place before a final appealable judgment in the underlying creditor case. Id. The court's reasoning is applicable to the improper transfers of estate assets in this case.
Neither the order invalidating the void transfers, nor an eviction under an order to manage real property, constitute executions. The orders of the Probate Court are enforceable. For the foregoing reasons plaintiff requests this Court enter summary judgment on the first cause in its favor.
III. DEPOSITION ADMISSIBILITY AND CORRECTION
Any facts relating to possible settlement negotiations asserted by defendants in their opposition brief, or contained on the record in the deposition of plaintiff's representative, are inadmissible and irrelevant to the first cause issue of possession. The portions in the deposition of plaintiff's representative, which are responses to

opposing counsel improperly asking questions regarding settlement, were properly objected to and should be stricken from the record.
Plaintiff notes that the representative of plaintiff issued a correction in the deposition errata clarifying that plaintiff has obtained insurance on the premises in questions.
IV. CONCLUSION
For the foregoing reasons, and in consideration of the evidence presented by both parties to the Court, plaintiff requests this Court find that there exists no genuine issue of material facts and plaintiff is entitled to judgment as a matter of law on the first cause for possession.

ASSIGNMENT OF ERRORS

1. THE TRIAL COURT ERRED, AS A MATTER OF LAW, WHEN IT GRANTED DEFENDANTS' MOTIONS FOR SUMMARY JUDGMENT.

First Issue Presented for Review and Argument

Whether Appellant was subject to the protection of the forcible entry and detainer statute, ORC 1923.01 et.seq., as an "occupier of land," or "tenant" requiring Appellees to serve Appellant with a three (3) day notice and file an eviction action in the Shaker Heights Municipal Court instead of effectuating a self help eviction.

Second Issue Presented for Review and Argument

Whether, as a matter of law, the trial court erred, by relying solely upon Ogle v. Disbrow, 2005 Ohio 4869 and the Ohio Landlord Tenant Act (5321.01 et.seq.) when determining whether Appellees' should have followed the Forcible Entry and Detainer statute (ORC 1923.01 et.seq.) and serve Appellant with a three (3) day notice and file an eviction action in the Shaker Heights Municipal Court to have Appellant removed from the residence.

I. STATEMENT OF THE CASE

Plaintiff's Amended Complaint filed on May 23, 2008 included ten Counts including the following causes of action:

Count One- Intentional Infliction of Emotional Distress

Count Two - Trespass

Count Three - Conversion

Count Four - Invasion of Privacy

Count Five - Ohio Landlord Tenant Act

Count Six - Violation of R.C. 5321.15

Count Seven - Wrongful Eviction

Count Eight - Punitive Damages

Count Nine - Breach of Contract

Count Ten - Unjust Enrichment

Defendant/Apppellee, Nancy Wallace answered the Amended Complaint. All Defendants moved for summary judgment and the trial court granted summary judgment to all Defendants in a Journal Entry dated January 27, 2009 which stated:

The Court, having considered all the evidence and having construed the evidence most strongly in favor of the non-moving party, determines that reasonable minds can come to but one conclusion, that there are no genuine issues of material fact, and that defendants are entitled to judgment as a matter of law. Plaintiff was not a tenant pursuant to Ohio's Landlord Tenant Act and no tenancy was created as a result of her employment. R.C. 5321.01 defines tenant as 'a person who has occupancy of residential premises." A person who lives in the residence of another without a rental agreement and who does not pay rent is not a tenant under Ohio law. See, Ogle v. Disbrow, 2005-Ohio-4869. No rental agreement was entered into between the parties. No rent was paid by the Plaintiff to the Defendants. In fact, Plaintiff paid rent to and maintained a residence separate from her place of employment. Therefore, Plaintiff's claims based on tenancy, Counts II, V, VI, and VII, must fail as a matter of law.

Plaintiff's claim for intentional infliction of emotional distress is denied as there was no outrageous conduct, as set forth in Pyle v. Pyle, (8th Dist. 1983) 11 Ohio App. 3d 31, 34. Plaintiff must establish that (1) the actor intended to cause emotional distress or knew or should have known serious emotional distress would result, (2) the conduct was so extreme and outrageous which goes "beyond all possible bounds of decency," (3) the actor's actions were the proximate cause of the plaintiff's psychic injury, and (4) Plaintiff's mental anguish suffered by Plaintiff is serious and of a nature that no reasonable person could be expected to endure it. There is no evidence that defendants intended or acted recklessly thus causing emotional distress. Plaintiff did not seek treatment from a psychiatrist, psychologist, counselor, or medical provider. No evidence has been presented to the Court of any behavior that would result in serious emotional distress or that any of the defendants' conduct was so extreme as to go "beyond all bounds of decency." Employees are fired daily and upon termination are removed from the premises either on their own or with an escort. Therefore, Count I is dismissed as a matter of law.

Plaintiff also alleges in Count IX a breach of employment contract claim. Under Ohio law, an at-will employment relationship is terminable at any time, by either party for any reason so long as it is not contrary to law. Mcintosh v. Roadway Express, (12th Dist. 1994) 94 Ohio App. 3d 195. There is no evidence that the parties entered into a written contract for employment. Plaintiff was an at-will employee. Therefore Count IX is dismissed as a matter of law.

Further, Plaintiff's conversion (Count III), invasion of privacy (Count IV), punitive damages (Count VIII) and unjust enrichment (Count X) claims also fail as a matter of law. Plaintiff has been given the opportunity to pick up any items left at Defendant, John Wallace's house and from opposing counsel, but has failed to do so. There is no evidence of misappropriation of personality, of improper publications, or of wrongful intrusion into Plaintiff's private activities. Plaintiff consented to Defendant's help to remove her items. No evidence has been submitted to the Court to allow for punitive damages. Finally, Plaintiff was fully compensated for her services and paid an additional week's salary. The Court grants Defendants' motions for summary judgment. Captioned matter is hereby dismissed. Court costs assessed to the Plaintiffs.

Pursuant to the grant of summary judgment, Plaintiff filed the within appeal on February 20, 2009, pursuant to which this matter is pending before this Court. Appellee, Nancy Wallace would point out to this Court that although Plaintiff has submitted a general assignment of error with respect to the granting of summary judgment as to all Defendants, the "issues presented for review" clarify that Plaintiff/Appellant has limited the within appeal the dismissal of the following:

Count Five - Ohio Landlord Tenant Act

Count Six - Violation of R.C. 5321.15

Count Seven - Wrongful Eviction

These counts of the Complaint do not involve the Appellee, Nancy Wallace as she did not possess an ownership interest in the property in question, nor did she possess any power as agent or attorney-in-fact of the owner, Defendant, John Wallace. Plaintiff has not chosen to assert any error with respect to the granting of summary judgment on those counts of the Amended Complaint that involved the actions of Nancy Wallace.

II.  STATEMENT OF FACTS

Appellant, Michelle Lee responded to an advertisement offering employment as a live-in caregiver to John F. Wallace at his home located at 3326 Braemer Road, Shaker Heights, Ohio. Appellee, Nancy Wallace (Mr. Wallace's daughter) interviewed Michelle Lee on August 15, 2007. Ms. Lee admits that she did not receive the job until she interviewed with Mr. Wallace, who decided to employ her. (Amended Complaint, Par. 6, Lee Dep. Pg. 59; Lee Dep. Pg. 142) She did not have a written employment contract. (Lee Dep. Pg. 62) Ms. Lee reported to Sally Wallace with respect to her employment. (Amended Complaint, Par. 8; Lee Dep. Pg. 67, 141) Ms. Lee understood that she was an employee-at-will and could be discharged at any time. (Lee Dep. Pg. 92)

On October 5, 2007, Michelle Lee was terminated from her at-will employment and asked to leave the premises. (Amended Complaint, Par. 13). Despite her allegations of being shocked, surprised, and emotionally distressed by the experience, in the two days preceding her discharge, she consulted with her current attorney; called a housing advocate in Shaker Heights about her rights; and bought a tape recorder with which she secretly tape recorded the entire incident. When the police arrived to assist in having Ms. Lee leave the premises, they determined that she did not have a valid driver's license. (Amended Complaint, Par. 21) At Ms. Lee's request, Defendant, Nancy Wallace assisted Michelle Lee in loading her belongings into her car and accompanied her to the storage unit where her furniture and other belongings were stored. (Amended Complaint, Par. 23) Any other personal items that Ms. Lee left at the Wallace residence, she refused to come back and retrieve.

The Amended Complaint alleges, "as a direct and proximate result of Defendants' actions, plaintiff became homeless, incurred attorney's fees, suffered extreme emotional distress, embarrassment and depression." (Amended Complaint, Par. 56) At deposition, Ms. Lee admitted that she has not paid any attorneys fees, but has a contingency contract. (Lee Dep. Pg. 213) Ms. Lee admitted that she was paid $ 1,100 on October 5, 2007 but chose to sleep in her car for two nights until she moved into an apartment on October 7, 2007. She could have stayed in a hotel, but chose not to. (Lee Dep. Pg. 215). Ms. Lee admitted that she has never sought any medical or psychological treatment for depression, anxiety, or emotional distress either before or after her discharge. (Lee Dep. Pg. 216).

Independent of this analysis, Ms. Lee has no cause of action against the Defendant/Appellee, Nancy Wallace, as she has failed to show that Nancy Wallace had any ownership interest in this property that would be a prerequisite to prosecuting any of the causes of action pending in the within appeal against Nancy Wallace.

III.  Law and Argument.

STANDARD OF REVIEW

A.  The appellate standard of review of a grant of summary judgment is de novo.

The standard of review of a grant or denial of summary judgment is the same for both a trial court and an appellate court. Lorain Natl. Bank v. Saratoga Apts., (1989) 61 Ohio App. 3d 127, 129. An appellate court considers an appeal from a summary judgment under a de novo standard of review. Grafton v. Ohio Edison Co., (1996) 77 Ohio St. 3d 102, 105. A de novo review requires an independent review of the trial court's decision without any deference to the trial court's determination. Id.; Brown v. Cty. Commrs., (1993) 87 Ohio App. 3d 704, 711. A grant of summary judgment will be upheld if "the pleadings, depositions, answers to interrogatories, written admissions, affidavits, transcripts of evidence in the pending case, and written stipulations of facts, if any, * * * show that there is no genuine issue as to any material fact" and, "construing the evidence most strongly in favor of the non-moving party, reasonable minds can only conclude that the moving party is entitled to judgment as a matter of law." Civ.R. 56 (C).

B.  The trial court properly granted summary judgment to Nancy Wallace on Counts V, VI, and VII of the Amended Complaint as neither Appellant nor the Nancy Wallace had any ownership interest in the property located at 3326 Braemar Road, Shaker Heights, Ohio.

Plaintiff never possessed a right of tenancy in the residence of John Wallace during the term of her at-will employment. An individual who lives in a residence with another person without a rental agreement and without the payment of rent is not a tenant and cannot maintain an action for wrongful eviction. Ogle v. Disbrow, (Ohio App. 6 Dist.) 2005-Ohio-4869, 2005 WL 2249582; Stone v. Cazeua, (Ohio App. 9 Dist.) 2007-Ohio-6213, 2007 WL 4146777; Amick v. Sickles, (Ohio App. 4 Dist.) 2008-Ohio-3913, 177 Ohio App. 3d 337. All of these courts held that the plaintiffs were merely a co-habitant of the home, with no rights under the Ohio Landlord Tenant Act as no rental agreement of any sort had been entered into and there was no payment of rent. With respect to Plaintiff's claims that the Defendants were required to use the procedures of the Forcible Entry and Detainer statutes, O.R.C. § 1923.01 et.seq. these courts specifically held that the protections of a tenant under Chapter 5321 of the Revised Code do not apply. See, Stone, supra, @ P 8-9; Amick, supra, @ 342.

When an employee occupies a portion of the premises of his employer, which occupancy is incidental to his employment, the relationship of landlord and tenant does not exist. Moreno v. Stahmann Farms, Inc., (10th Cir. 1982) 693 F. 2d 106,107; Anderson v. William J. Davis, Inc., (D.C. App. 1989) 553 A. 2d 648,649. Once the employment relationship ends, so does any right to be on the premises of the employer. See, Miracle v. Stewart, (KY.1939) 278 Ky. 158, 128 S.W. 2d 613, 615. See also, Bethany Reformed Church v. Hager, (Ill App. Ct. 1st Dist. 1980) 406 N.E.2d 93; North Am. Old Roman Catholic Church v. Bernadette, (Ill.App. Ct. 1st Dist. 1992) 627 N.E. 2d 1094; Muniz v. Kravis, (Conn. App. Ct. 2000)757 A. 2d 1207.

In this instance, Ms. Lee (1) was an employee-at-will who could be discharged at any time; (2) had no rental agreement; and (3) did not pay rent Ms. Lee has admitted that Nancy Wallace did not control the terms of her at-will employment. (Amended Complaint, Par. 8; Lee Dep. Pg. 67, 141) Moreover, there is no evidence that Defendant, Nancy Wallace possessed any ownership interest in the residence in question and had no power over any alleged tenancy rights being claimed by the Appellant.

C.  Conclusion

Based on the foregoing, Appellee, Nancy Wallace requests that this Court affirm the granting of summary judgment in favor of the Appellee.

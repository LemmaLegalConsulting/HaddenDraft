MEMORANDUM IN OPPOSITION TO EVICTION

HARRISON v. WILLOW CREEK ESTATES

Statement of Facts
Harrison is a tenant at Willow Creek Estates, where she has resided since January 10, 2020. On July 15, 2022, Harrison requested a reasonable accommodation due to her disability, asking for a short extension to clean her unit and offering to accept cleaning assistance. Despite this request, Willow Creek Estates proceeded with eviction proceedings on August 1, 2022, citing Harrison's failure to maintain her unit.

Argument
42 U.S.C. § 3604(f)(3)(B) requires housing providers to make reasonable accommodations in rules, policies, practices, or services when necessary to afford a person with a disability equal opportunity to use and enjoy a dwelling. Harrison's disability meets the governing definition. Willow Creek Estates was aware of her disability and the need for accommodation through her July 15, 2022 request. Willow Creek Estates asserts no direct-threat, undue-burden, or fundamental-alteration defense.

The requested accommodation directly relates to Harrison's disability and the conduct that led to the eviction, as the cleaning assistance would have addressed the housekeeping issues cited in the eviction notice, thereby affording Harrison equal use and enjoyment of her dwelling.

Harrison requested a facially reasonable accommodation by asking for a short extension and offering to accept cleaning assistance. Willow Creek Estates denied her request by proceeding with eviction on August 1, 2022.

Conclusion
The court should rule in favor of Harrison, finding that Willow Creek Estates failed to comply with 42 U.S.C. § 3604(f)(3)(B).
